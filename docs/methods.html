Who is waiting for Mexico’s grid

U.S. vs PRC vs Mexico, in megawatts and days. Diagnostic only — no policy advice.

Dashboard Methods Bibliography

What this measures

U.S. and PRC-tied projects in Mexico compete for the same grid. What you can count is who is waiting, in megawatts and days: place in CENACE’s published interconnection queue (power plants) and connection queue (factories). It is not industrial-park announcements, not IMMEX clocks, and not water titles. The clock is 2026–2036. Nothing here is an influence score, and nothing here is a recommendation.

CENACE still does not name the applicant. CNE names generation permit-holders, including country of origin. Unique matches of that permit book onto plants still in line are the U.S. vs PRC scoreboard. Factories stay mostly unnamed.

What CENACE publishes

CENACE posts workbooks of the interconnection queue (generators, registry prefix SICE) and the connection queue (load, prefix SCCC). The live listing is the year dropdown on CENACE’s connection and interconnection request page. Directory indexes return 403; files are retrieved from the ASP.NET listing. Filenames use the accented Spanish words for interconnection and connection.

Columns in the 2021–2026 workbooks: registry number, request date, status, status date, estimated normal-operation date, capacity (MW), technology, municipality, state, and regional control office. Company name is not in that list. There is also no substation interconnection-point field. Geography on the map is the municipality of record, not a bus name.

Study-status values observed, shown in English on the dashboard: received / under review, accepted, observations, attended, cancelled, and (in older files) under review. “Still in line” on this site means the first three plus under review. Attended is already processed. Cancelled is cancelled.

CNE permit join (power plants)

The public file generation permits in force, by modality names the permit-holder, authorized MW, technology, state, address, lead company, and country of origin. We join that book onto in-queue SICE rows only when the fingerprint is unique on both sides.

  • Key: state + MW rounded to one decimal + technology family (solar/PV, wind, combined cycle, hydro, thermal, other).
  • A tighter key adds municipality when both sides have it.
  • Collisions stay unnamed. We do not pick the “closest” permit.
  • Country coding: United States → U.S.; China → PRC; Mexico → Mexico. Mixed labels such as Canada and China are Other, not PRC.
  • The hand codebook wins on the same registry id. Load (SCCC) is codebook-only.

Queue MW often does not equal authorized permit MW, so most plants stay unnamed. Zero PRC unique matches is a finding of this rule, not a fill-in.

Special-purpose vehicle hole: country of origin can hide PRC (or U.S.) capital behind a Mexican company. That hole stays on the Mexico or unnamed bars. We do not guess beneficial owners from park announcements.

Factories (load)

CENACE does not name factories. Only cited codebook rows are colored: Tesla in Santa Catarina (probable U.S.) and LGMG in Marin (probable PRC). Hofusan in Salinas Victoria is in the bibliography and not in the codebook: El Economista names the park, not megawatts or a registry id. The rest of load megawatts stay unnamed.

Days waiting

For each row, days waiting is snapshot date minus request date (floored at zero). Snapshot date is parsed from the title row that states the update date (for example 28 February 2026), not from file mtime. Rows with MW ≥ 5,000 are dropped from totals as unit or digit errors.
